What shareholders own

A fictional five-year commercial issuer receives a limited license from a player-controlled brand owner. Investors finance 100% of that issuer’s equity across 100,000 units. The player retains the underlying brand rights and receives upfront and ongoing licensing/service payments.

Quarterly payouts equal 90% of positive after-tax profit; losses consume cash reserves. Remaining cash is distributed once at the end of year five. There is no guaranteed principal or terminal earnings multiple.

Offering proceeds fund reserves, issuance costs and the upfront license. Licenses, distributions, offering terms and any secondary trading would require negotiated agreements and legal review.

Cross-sectional comparison

Profiles and sales budgets are fictional. Equity values discount assumed distributable cash at 12%; the implied expected return is set by construction, not discovered alpha.

Discount-rate sensitivity

Cash flows are held fixed, including the baseline 4% reserve-interest assumption. This is a discount-rate-only comparison. Valuation figures use the original 100,000-unit issue basis, independent of paper-account splits.

Who earns money, and from what?

Separate operating cash generated by businesses from money exchanged between investors. Share-price gains are uncertain; each seller’s proceeds are funded by a buyer.

Initial financing

Investors fund the operating reserve, a 2% issuance cost and a negotiated upfront IP license payment. The model solves the maximum residual license payment at its assumed equity valuation; this is not evidence the player would accept it.

Operating cash recipients

The 5% conditional league/team rights cost is an assumed expense only. It implies neither permission nor salary-cap compliance. All scenarios require independently approved rights and related-party terms.

Platform operating scenario

Assumed equal mix of six fictional ventures; $2.4m annual fixed operating budget plus $15,000 per issuer. Trading is conditional on an authorized venue, 20% active float and a 5 bp platform fee. Paper-account commissions are 10 bp and are a separate simulation assumption. Customer acquisition, legal costs and actual demand are not validated.

Rights and revenue diligence register

This is an illustrative diligence specification. No actual license, sponsor contract, audit, athlete agreement or regulatory approval has been obtained.

Futures and option markets

Separate collateral from cost, and exposure from account equity. All contracts settle in paper cash.

Cash-settled futures

The carry approximation uses the cash rate and conditional expected distributions, not certain risk-free payouts. Variation margin moves cash each session; futures notional is never counted as an asset. Final settlement uses spot.

European options

Premium paid in full. Black–Scholes with a continuous-yield approximation; European exercise only. Discrete distributions, jumps and stock-loan frictions violate the model’s ideal assumptions. Greeks are teaching sensitivities and become unreliable near the final cash liquidation. No naked option writing.

Why an arbitrage signal is not a free profit

Differences between model value, spot quotes and futures carry can reflect transaction costs, unavailable borrow, binding margin, uncertain cash distributions and market impact. The business cash model and market-price process are separate. Neither calibrated arbitrage relationships nor executable external counterparties are claimed.

Model risk and joint uncertainty

20,000 paired five-year scenarios. Distributions are judgmental assumptions; these are not fitted probabilities, historical returns or a backtest.

Portfolio concentration

Equal-dollar allocations at modeled issue values. Scenario present-value losses are measured against initial funding, discounted at the same required return; they are not one-day trading VaR.

Uncertainty intervals

Ranges show modeled P5/P50/P95 present values per unit, not statistical confidence intervals for a fitted estimate.

Market specification and boundaries

This is an executable research prototype with explicit simplifications. It is not a brokerage, exchange, clearinghouse, securities offering, or reproduction of every production-market rule.

How the two simulators connect

The commercial model supplies opening valuations and a fixed schedule of expected quarterly cash. Synthetic trading quotes follow a separate process. If a realized distribution would exceed its cum-distribution quote, the engine logs an explicit information-repricing gain before the cash transfer. At final liquidation the price becomes zero. This bridge is a material simplifying assumption, not an arbitrage-free pricing model.

Legal and structural feasibility

The 2023 NBA CBA, Article II §13(d), prohibits assignment of team-compensation rights. This candidate design finances a separate licensed business. That does not establish legal permission: CBA anti-circumvention, related-party transactions, athlete publicity rights, team and league IP, securities issuance and secondary trading all require review.

Fantex previously issued athlete-linked tracking stock in other sports. That precedent establishes that athlete-income finance is not a new invention; it does not establish NBA permission. A commercial offering would require a permissible underlying agreement and appropriate securities, derivatives, brokerage and trading arrangements.

Performance is not a cash dividend. Trades generally move the existing player contract to another team. Contract extensions, bonuses, options, guarantees, award eligibility and renegotiations have specific conditions. This prototype does not claim to implement the entire CBA.

Research questions that can be falsified

  1. Does sponsor coverage reduce downside after accounting for fixed costs and counterparty default?
  2. What share of modeled equity value comes from operating distributions versus liquidation cash?
  3. How much downside survives diversification because commercial demand is shared?
  4. When does a favorable valuation view fail after spread, borrow fees, margin and dividend obligations?
  5. Can a short squeeze or funding shock cause liquidation before a valuation thesis can play out?
  6. How sensitive is the result to adverse selection among ventures willing to issue equity?

Research, pitch and reproducibility

Project thesis

Court Capital connects player-licensed commercial businesses to an auditable cash-flow model and a paper securities market. The proposed differentiation is a bridge from licensed rights and signed customer contracts to distributable cash, valuation and financial risk.

The proposed first business is financial reporting and servicing for athlete commercial ventures. Securities issuance and secondary trading are later, partner-dependent stages. Fantex, Vestible and Finlete are precedents; athlete investment itself is not a new invention.

Recruiter demonstration

  1. Compare an emerging athlete’s commercial equity value with a mature venture.
  2. Buy or short paper equity, then apply injury and rate scenarios.
  3. Advance through a distribution date to see long income and short obligations.
  4. Compare a future and a call option under the same price shock.
  5. Reconcile account equity and examine both portfolio downside and the platform’s break-even assumptions.
